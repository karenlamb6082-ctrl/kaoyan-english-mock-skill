# Mock 01-04 Ecology Ledger

Read this reference before designing Mock 05 or when auditing theme diversity, conflict repetition, difficulty, item mix, or writing overlap in the current series.

## Overall Finding

Mock 01 established structure, Mock 02 improved policy boundaries, Mock 03 produced the strongest full-paper ecology, and Mock 04 produced the strongest individual items. The remaining weakness is cross-paper and within-paper sameness: different surface topics repeatedly resolve into measurement vs value, formal oversight vs real control, universal systems vs marginal groups, and technology benefits constrained by institutional governance.

Do not make the next paper harder by increasing rare vocabulary or abstract nouns. Increase evidence span, viewpoint boundaries, author attitude, and distractor precision.

## Three Principal Problems

1. Topic deduplication does not yet remove conflict and argument-template repetition.
2. Increasing difficulty has sometimes meant increasing abstraction instead of evidence distance.
3. Review proves that correct options work but does not yet prove that every wrong option is competitive and uniquely wrong.

## Mock 05 Blueprint

| Text | Domain | Topic | Conflict | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | social/consumer institutions | cashless venues and payment choice | convenience vs choice and system resilience | 2 easy + 3 medium |
| 2 | environment/science | assisted migration | ecological caution vs climate inaction risk | 1 easy + 3 medium + 1 hard |
| 3 | culture/history | post-disaster reconstruction | material authenticity vs communal memory and continuity | 1 easy + 3 medium + 1 hard |
| 4 | economic institutions/technology markets | platform interoperability | integrated experience/security vs exit freedom/competition | 1 easy + 1 medium + 3 hard |

Use four different discourse modes: narrative-led news commentary, popular science controversy, historical-cultural commentary, and institutional criticism. Do not make the four articles share the same compromise ending.

Use this full-paper state:

- Cloze: why maps require deliberate distortion.
- Part B: information matching, five skilled-trade workers on apprenticeship mentoring, seven statements with two boundary-based extras.
- Translation: history of standardized time and its effects on railways, commerce, and daily life.
- Part A Writing: campus film-screening venue cancellation and alternative arrangements.
- Part B Writing: fully booked but physically unused sports facilities; improved use after wait-list and cancellation reminders.

For Writing Part B, require the observable contradiction, causes such as low-cost reservation and scarcity anxiety, and both individual cancellation responsibility and institutional wait-list/reminder design.
